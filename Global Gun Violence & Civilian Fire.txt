Global Gun Violence & Civilian Firearms (2000–2023)
Three tidy tables that answer one question from many angles: how many guns are out there, how many people are killed with them, and what else (wealth, inequality, youth, cities) goes with it?

File	Rows	Grain
firearm_homicides_country_year.csv	3,120	country × year, 2000–2024
civilian_firearms_2017.csv	230	country / territory (Small Arms Survey 2017)
country_gun_profile.csv	217	one row per country — ready for a scatter plot or a map
Firearm homicide figures are available for 99 countries; total homicide rates for many more.

Key columns
homicide_rate, firearm_homicide_rate (per 100k), firearm_homicides (count) — UNODC.
firearm_share_of_homicides_pct — what % of murders are committed with a gun.
firearms_per_100_people — civilian gun ownership (Small Arms Survey 2017).
Context from the World Bank: population, GDP per capita, Gini, unemployment, urbanisation, share of men aged 15–64, military spending, secondary-school enrolment.

Fun fact to test in your notebook: across 95 countries the Spearman correlation between guns per 100 people and the firearm homicide rate is 0.00 — practically no relationship. Serbia, Switzerland and Finland hold 28–39 guns per 100 people yet have very few gun murders, while Jamaica and El Salvador hold 9–12 and have some of the highest rates on Earth. Inequality? Organised crime? Policing? Find out.

Ideas for notebooks

Notes
Homicide = intentional homicide (UNODC ICCS definition); suicides and accidents are not included.
Missing values mean the source has no figure for that year — they are not zero.
Weapon (mechanism) reporting is incomplete in some countries: e.g. Brazil's UNODC firearm counts cover only part of its homicides, so its firearm_share_of_homicides_pct is understated. Compare with homicide_rate.
Gun ownership numbers are estimates with wide uncertainty; check computation_method.
